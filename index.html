The Trajectory

3-1. The Execution

Theory is obsolete without execution. I refused to be confined to theory-centric lectures. I wanted to build.

  • The Genesis of Leadership: Rallied students to build a 2-stage water rocket with payload ejection. I promised my team: "I will put at least 100,000 KRW in your hands." We won 3rd place, and I kept that promise.
  • Ground-Level Mechanics: Acquired a heavy commercial vehicle license and the national Traffic Accident Appraiser license to understand physical limits and collisions.
  • The Masterclass in Team Building: Took a gap year to perfect the rocket. We failed multiple launches. Working Monday to Friday, 10 AM to 7 PM, I fostered absolute trust, bought meals, and embraced rational debate. I personally engineered the separation mechanism.
  • Redefining Braking Systems: Won 1st place with an SUA prevention device. Used fluid resistance to decelerate the driveshaft continuously, proving it with a 3D-printed torque rig.
  • The Global Reality: Winning 3rd place at ICDF was overshadowed by seeing Chinese undergrads win 1st. I felt the chilling reality of their tech acceleration. My mission crystalized: Tech Patriotism.
  • Pushing Propulsion: Engineered a shuttered side thruster for guided projectiles, verifying theoretical thrust via Ansys Fluent.

3-2. The Crash

I targeted US defense giants like Shield AI and Anduril. Then, I hit the Valley of Death.

  • The Valley of Death: Failed the Preliminary Startup Package. Clashed with my co-founder. Bombarded with skepticism: "Where is your business model?" Overwhelmed, I pivoted to a bird-inspired drone to appease critics.
  • The Cost of Compromise: Lost 13kg and suffered severe insomnia. I lost the joy of engineering trying to satisfy Korean mentors who insisted on "Customers and BMs" instead of targeting VCs aligned with extreme hardware.
  • The Rebirth: At the SNAAC Ideathon, my team was paralyzed over a microbe wastewater BM. Biology isn't my field, but I focused for 30 minutes, synthesized the logic, and delivered a legendary pitch. We won 1st place in two days.
  • 15 Hardware Crashes: Poured my 5M KRW savings into 11 iterations of the Ornithopter. Conducted 15+ flights. All failed. It was pure heuristic engineering—surviving shattered frames and negative Gs.

3-3. The Validation

Execution is the only metric that matters.

  • Hacking the System: Enraged by the lack of support for hardware builders after a KBS doc, I marched into the SNU Engineering Dean's office and demanded backing. This directly led to the SNU Entrepreneurial Innovation Program.
  • The Ultimate Proof: At ADEX 2025, I saw a drone-launched guided projectile mockup at the LIG Nex1 booth, partnered with Shield AI. It was my exact concept from 10 months prior. Had I built the prototype, the PoC would be mine. Lesson learned: Execution over everything.
  • The Next Lap: The Ornithopter is back on the bench. Using DeLaurier's model, I am reaching the edge of PoC. Project "AeroRover" is underway. Join me on the track.
