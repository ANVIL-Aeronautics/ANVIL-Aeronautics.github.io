[Block 3: Story (The "Crash & Respond" Trajectory)]
3-1. The Execution
Theory is obsolete without execution. In my junior year, I refused to be confined to theory-centric lectures. I wanted to build.

The Genesis of Leadership (Jun 2023): I rallied students and founded an engineering club to build a 2-stage water rocket with payload ejection. I made my team one absolute promise: "I will put at least 100,000 KRW in your hands." We won 3rd place at the Creative Design Fair, and I kept that promise.

Ground-Level Mechanics (2023): To understand the extremes of physical machines and collisions, I acquired a heavy commercial vehicle license and the national Traffic Accident Appraiser license.

The Masterclass in Team Building (2024): I took a gap year to perfect the rocket. We failed multiple launches—fins shattered, separation mechanisms jammed. But we worked Monday to Friday, 10 AM to 7 PM. I fostered absolute trust, bought the meals, and embraced every rational debate. I personally engineered the separation mechanism, while we collectively handled parachute terminal velocity, fluid dynamics, and the brutal reality of 3D printing tolerances.

Redefining Braking Systems (Sep 2024): Combining my traffic investigation background with engineering, I won 1st place with a Sudden Unintended Acceleration (SUA) prevention device. Instead of brake pads, I used fluid resistance and viscosity to decelerate the driveshaft continuously. I built a 3D-printed torque rig to prove the RPM drop.

The Global Reality (Nov 2024): Winning 3rd place at the ICDF was overshadowed by seeing Chinese undergrads from Harbin Institute of Technology (a US-sanctioned entity) win 1st with a satellite docking system. I felt the chilling reality of their tech acceleration. My life's mission crystalized: Tech Patriotism, aligning directly with US national interests.

Pushing Propulsion (Late 2024): I ran flow analyses on hypersonic engine inlets and engineered a shuttered side thruster for guided projectiles. I verified the theoretical thrust increase via Ansys Fluent.

3-2. The Crash
I wanted to build a drone-launched small guided projectile. An Army specialist told me: "Build a prototype, and we will test it." My target was US defense giants like Shield AI and Anduril. Then, I hit the Valley of Death.

The Valley of Death (Early 2025): I failed. I clashed with my co-founder of 10 years and failed the Preliminary Startup Package. I was bombarded with skepticism: "Does it make money? What’s your business model?" Overwhelmed, I pivoted to a bird-inspired drone to appease the critics.

The Cost of Compromise: I lost 13kg and suffered severe insomnia. I had lost the joy of engineering and became obsessed with results. Korean mentors insisted on focusing on "Customers, BMs, and Pain Points." I tried to satisfy everyone instead of targeting VCs aligned with my extreme hardware vision.

The Rebirth (Jun 2025): I rebuilt my mental architecture through Christian faith. At the SNAAC Ideathon, my team was paralyzed trying to build a BM for microbe wastewater treatment. Biology is not my field, but I focused for 30 minutes, synthesized the core logic, and delivered a legendary pitch. We won 1st place in two days. I traded 13kg of weight loss for ultimate resilience.

15 Hardware Crashes: I poured my 5M KRW savings into building up to the 11th iteration of the Ornithopter. I conducted 15+ flights over 8 months. All failed. It was pure heuristic engineering—fixing motor torque, surviving shattered frames, and enduring negative Gs when the aircraft inverted.

The Internship: Out of funds, I joined Airbility (a drone startup) to build anti-drone net gun systems, running MATLAB calculations and learning organizational management.

3-3. The Validation
Execution is the only metric that matters.

Hacking the System (Sep 2025): I saw a KBS documentary highlighting China's engineering obsession versus Korea's medical school obsession. Enraged by the lack of support for hardware builders, I marched into the SNU Engineering Dean's office and demanded backing for visionary students. This directly led to the SNU Entrepreneurial Innovation Program, where I operate today. In a media interview, I proudly cited Palmer Luckey as my role model.

The Ultimate Proof (Oct 2025): At ADEX 2025, I walked into the LIG Nex1 booth. There it was—a drone-launched small guided projectile mockup, partnered with Shield AI. It was the exact concept I had pitched 10 months prior. My technological thesis was 100% accurate. Had I ignored the critics and just built the prototype, the PoC would have been mine. Lesson learned: Execution over everything.

The Next Lap (2026): I brought the Ornithopter back to the bench. Despite manufacturing hurdles like shaft play and manual tolerances, I am using DeLaurier's model to calculate lift and thrust. I am reaching the edge of PoC. Simultaneously, Project "AeroRover" is underway. The journey restarts here. Join me on the track.
